**I am interested in how financial valuation happens, and how it impacts on democratic politics.**

My interest in the techno-political side of finance stems in part from work on the governing of Norwegian oil resources. Together with Kristin Asdal, I have written about how **Norway's oil reserves came to be governed as a financialised asset.**

A consequence of introducing financial valuation tools into the governing of oil was the establishment of Norway's so-called 'oil fund', officially named the Government Pension Fund Global (GPFG).

- Asdal & Lahn (2024), **[Governing as valuing: Assetization and the making of the Norwegian oil fund](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0308518X241260588).** _Environment and Planning A: Economy and Space_.

Today, GPFG is the world's largest Sovereign Wealth Fund. Through the project [Fossil Free Futures](https://www.su.se/english/research/research-catalogue/research-projects/b/fossil-free-futures-divestment-across-the-nordic-countries-) I have been investigating how GPFG assesses the environmental impacts of its investments, and how it seeks to mitigate climate risk through what the fund's managers call 'active ownership'. Publications based on this research are forthcoming.

I have also done research on the technology of Bitcoin and how bitcoin-mining is used to re-value fossil fuels that were though to be 'stranded', i.e. without value. This research is forthcoming in the journal _Valuation Studies_.
